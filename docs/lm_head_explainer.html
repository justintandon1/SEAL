Hidden states vs lm_head

The one idea that makes it click: when you extract, the text is already written — so there's nothing to predict. Flip the switch below and watch what the model actually needs to do.

1. Same model, two totally different jobs

The exact same model can be used two ways. The difference isn't the model — it's whether you're writing new text or just reading finished text. That single difference is why you can drop lm_head.

2. So what do you keep, and what do you skip?

In extraction mode, the answer becomes obvious. You never asked "what word comes next" — the words are already there. You only asked "what was the model thinking as it read." That thinking = the hidden state.

✓ What you NEED

The layer-20 hidden state. It's the model's internal representation of the reasoning. Your steering vector is literally built by averaging these for correct vs. incorrect traces and subtracting.

It's produced partway through the transformer — before lm_head ever runs.

✗ What you SKIP

lm_head and its 150,000 next-word scores. Its only job is predicting the next token so the model can keep writing.

You're not writing anything — the trace is finished. So this step produces a giant tensor you'd immediately throw away. That tensor is what overflowed your 24 GB.

3. The analogy that matches your task

You hand a student a finished essay and ask: "As you read this, which parts make your brain go 'wait, let me double-check'?"

✓ You scan their brain while they read → that's grabbing the hidden state. This is all you want.

✗ You do NOT ask them to write the next paragraph of the essay → that's lm_head. The essay is already done, so composing 150,000 candidate next-sentences is pure wasted effort — and it's what filled up the desk (your VRAM).

Your fix model.model(...) says: "read it and let me scan your brain — but don't write anything." Same brain scan, none of the waste.
