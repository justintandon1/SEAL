Building R_iso: the random-vector control

Control A from the progress report, specified end to end. The vector itself is four lines of arithmetic — the work is in the decisions around it: what magnitude, how many draws, and what makes a flat result mean something rather than nothing. No GPU is needed to build or verify it.

1What this control has to rule out

§11 of the progress report established the mechanism: on all six benchmarks the budget-limited pile — problems where the baseline hit the 10,000-token cap — supplies at least 100% of the net gain, while the finished pile contributes between −4.2 and +0.6 points. On MATH-500 with S_combo, the 164 capped problems go 1.22% → 51.22% while the 336 finished ones go 95.24% → 93.15%.

So the measured effect is compression, not better reasoning. That is precisely why this control is on the critical path: a norm-57 vector added at layer 20 at every generated position could plausibly shorten chains of thought regardless of which direction it points. Build this control to be decisive, not to be survived.

2The recipe

Recipe: R_iso = 57.06 · g / ‖g‖, where g ~ N(0, I₁₅₃₆) drawn from a pinned seed. Applied at layer 20, coef = −1.0, --remove_bos — identical in every respect to the SEAL arms except the direction.

Three draws, seeds 1 / 2 / 3, written to results/control/R_iso_seed{1,2,3}.pt. Each is a bare 1536-dim float32 tensor plus a sidecar .meta.json, matching what build_combo_vector.py emits — the loader at eval_MATH_steering.py:190 is just torch.load(..., weights_only=True), so no special format is required.

3Decision 1 — why norm 57.06

The intervention is hidden_states[flag, -1] += coef · vector. At a fixed coef = −1.0, the vector's norm is the intervention strength, so a control at the wrong magnitude tests nothing: too small and it is inert by construction, too large and it degrades the model for reasons unrelated to direction.

57.06 is the mean of the three domain-vector norms — the same mean-source target S_combo already uses. Measured from the committed vectors:

The five real vectors span 55.29–59.67, a spread of 8%. Landing the control in the middle of that band means any difference in outcome cannot be attributed to strength.

4Decision 2 — why three draws, not one

A single random vector is n = 1. If it comes back flat you cannot separate "random directions do nothing here" from "that particular draw happened to do nothing," and if it comes back hot you cannot tell a real effect from one unlucky draw. Neither outcome is publishable from one sample.

Three seeds also settles the sign question for free. A contrast vector has a meaningful orientation — S points from execution toward reflection, and coef = −1.0 pushes against it. A random vector has no such orientation, so "should we also run +1.0?" is not a real question: −R_seed1 is drawn from exactly the same distribution as R_seed2. Three draws at −1.0 already sample both half-spaces.

5Decision 3 — isotropic is the easy control

This is the part most worth arguing about in the writeup. The residual stream is strongly anisotropic: activations concentrate in a low-dimensional region with a few dominant directions. An isotropic Gaussian draw is very nearly orthogonal to that region, so R_iso may come back flat for a reason that has nothing to do with reasoning — it simply never enters the subspace the model computes in.

That does not make R_iso useless. It is the pre-registered spec, it is the standard control in the steering literature, and it is what a reader will ask for first. But its flatness is weak evidence on its own, and the writeup should say so rather than claim more than the design supports.

The harder follow-on: R_cov. Draw from a Gaussian matched to the covariance of layer-20 hidden states at boundary tokens, rescale to 57.06 as above. This lands the control inside the region the model actually occupies, so a flat result there is a genuine statement about direction. It needs the hidden.pt activations, which live on the pod rather than in the repo — which is why it is staged second, not because it matters less.

Recommended order: build and run R_iso first (cheap, expected, pre-registered), and hold R_cov as the immediate follow-on if R_iso is flat. If R_iso is not flat, R_cov becomes unnecessary — the domain story is already in trouble and the budget should go to the coefficient sweep instead.

6The geometry check, and what it can't catch

Before spending GPU time, confirm each draw is actually unrelated to the real vectors. In d = 1536 dimensions the cosine between a random direction and any fixed vector concentrates hard around zero: the standard deviation is 1/√d = 0.0255. Measured over 20,000 draws against S_math: mean +0.0001, sd 0.0251, matching theory.

Pre-register the redraw rule, don't improvise it. Reject and redraw any seed with |cos| > 0.08 against any of the five vectors, and record the rejection in the meta. Across three draws the chance of triggering this by chance is about 0.6%, so it will almost certainly never fire — which is the point. A discard rule invented after seeing a hot result is the same rule with none of the credibility.

What this check cannot catch: near-orthogonality to the SEAL vectors does not imply near-orthogonality to whatever direction actually drives compression. If the effect is carried by a generic "wrap up and answer" direction that the SEAL vectors merely overlap with, a random draw could still touch it. That possibility is what the evaluation, not the geometry, has to settle.

7The script

New file build_random_vector.py, mirroring the structure and metadata conventions of build_combo_vector.py. 

# Primary: three isotropic draws at the mean-source norm.
for s in 1 2 3; do
  python build_random_vector.py \
    --mode iso --seed $s \
    --like results/general/S_combo.pt \
    --check-against results/general/S_combo.pt \
    --check-against vectors/apps_v_code.pt \
    --check-against vectors/logiqa_v_logic.pt \
    --out results/control/R_iso_seed$s.pt
done

8Three things that will bite

Do not reuse check_sign_convention

build_combo_vector.py aborts if any pair of input vectors has a negative cosine — correct there, because summing oppositely-oriented contrast vectors cancels signal. A random vector will sit at cos ≈ 0 and will be negative about half the time. Importing that guard here would reject roughly half of all valid draws, and worse, it would do so silently biasing the control toward the SEAL vectors' half-space. Reuse cosine(), not the guard.

Pin the generator explicitly

Use torch.Generator().manual_seed(s) passed into torch.randn, not the global RNG. Global seeding makes the output depend on whatever else consumed random numbers first, which quietly breaks reproducibility the moment the script grows a second draw. Generate on CPU in float32; the forward pass casts to the activation dtype at modeling_qwen2.py:884.

Normalize before scaling, and check the norm after

A raw randn(1536) has norm ≈ √1536 ≈ 39.2, not 1 and not 57.06. Unit-normalize first, then multiply. Assert the final norm is within 1e-3 of target before writing — a silent factor-of-39 error would look exactly like "random steering destroys the model."

10Running it, and the readouts

Three runs total: three seeds, MATH-500 only. Not three seeds across all six benchmarks. The baseline arm already exists and is reused, so the control costs three new evaluations, nothing else.

Why one benchmark is enough

The full grid would be 3 vectors × 6 benchmarks = 18 runs, on the order of the entire ~38 GPU-h already spent on the study. Doubling the project's compute for a control is the wrong trade, and the extra 15 runs would not change the conclusion. Three reasons MATH-500 carries it alone:

Test a control where the effect is strongest. This is the load-bearing argument. MATH-500 has the largest measured gain in the study at +15.0. If a magnitude-matched random direction cannot reproduce the biggest effect, it will not reproduce the small ones — APPS is +1.2 to +2.6, LogiQA +2.6 to +4.0. A pass on the strongest benchmark generalizes downward on its own; a pass on APPS would generalize nowhere, because an arm that moves nothing is unsurprising where almost nothing moves.

The mechanism is most visible there. §11 attributes the gain to recovering baseline-truncated problems, so the control needs a benchmark with enough truncation for that channel to be observable. MATH-500 sits at a 32.8% baseline cap rate with 164 capped and 336 finished problems — both piles large enough to decompose. GSM8K, at 14.9%, would give a capped pile too small to read.

It is the cheapest of the six. APPS runs at batch_size 1 with 8,893 average tokens and an 83.6% cap rate, costing several times a MATH-500 arm. Picking the most informative benchmark and the cheapest benchmark happens to be the same choice here.

Comparability is free as well: all five SEAL vectors already have MATH-500 coverage on the same rand42_500 selection, so the control arms drop straight into the existing table and the existing paired tests.

Expand only if something moves. If all three seeds are flat, the control is finished — report it and move to Control B. If a seed comes back hot, extend that seed to the other benchmarks: one vector across more benchmarks, never three. The wide budget gets spent once there is a result that justifies it, not in advance on the chance that there might be. 
# One run per seed. Baseline is reused, not regenerated.
for s in 1 2 3; do
  RUN_BASELINE=0 DATASETS=math RESULT_ROOT=results/results_for_control_vectors \
    scripts/eval_steering_vector_benchmarks.sh \
      results/control/R_iso_seed$s.pt R_iso_seed$s 0
done
Reuse the baseline, but verify the pairing first. RUN_BASELINE=0 points the arm at the existing MATH-500 baseline rather than burning an hour regenerating it. That is only valid if SAMPLE_SEED=42, MATH_MAX_EXAMPLES=500 and MAX_TOKENS=10000 match the existing arm exactly — the exact paired McNemar test depends on identical problem ordering, and a mismatch produces a number that looks fine and means nothing.

The magnitude sweep is not optional

If all three seeds come back flat, that result is ambiguous between "the direction is what matters" and "a norm-57 perturbation at layer 20 does approximately nothing, whatever it is." Take seed 1 to COEF=-2, -4, -8 on 100 problems and find where output visibly degrades. Establishing that the intervention site is live is what converts a flat R_iso from an absence of evidence into evidence. Roughly one GPU-hour.

Report the same five things the SEAL arms report

  1. Accuracy vs baseline, with the exact paired McNemar test
  2. Mean tokens and percent change
  3. Cap-hit rate change
  4. The §11 capped / finished decomposition — the one that actually decides it
  5. Cosine against all five vectors, carried over from the build meta

Wiring: add one arm per benchmark to the BENCHMARKS dict at scripts/report_stats.py:65 pointing at results_for_control_vectors/...; the baseline entries stay untouched. Then python scripts/report_stats.py.

Decision rule, fixed in advance. Call R_iso flat if Δaccuracy is within ±2 points and Δtokens within ±5% on all three seeds. Any seed above +5 points is a live positive result: stop, extend that seed to full benchmark coverage, and treat the domain-specificity claim as unsupported until it is resolved. Anything in between — say a consistent +3 with −15% tokens — is the genuinely awkward case, and it should be reported as a partial pass with the capped/finished split shown, not rounded to either verdict.

11What this does and doesn't answer

It answers: whether a magnitude-matched perturbation at the same site and coefficient reproduces the compression and the cap-recovery gain. That is the load-bearing question for every domain-specificity claim in the report.

It does not answer whether the SEAL recipe is reasoning-specific. A random direction and a direction built by the same contrast procedure on non-reasoning text are different objects, and the second is Control B. Nor does it speak to whether the boundary labels carry the signal — that is the shuffled-label control from the research plan, which is arguably the sharpest of the three and is still unscheduled.

The pre-registered interpretation grid stands: both controls flat with the SEAL vectors at +12–15 means reasoning-specific; B gains while A stays flat means the recipe generalizes; both gaining means the effect is a generic length-and-layer artifact and the domain story collapses.
